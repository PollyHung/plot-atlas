# Subject Taxonomy

Two levels: **6 areas** (5 substantive + 1 cross-cutting) and **143 nested subject tags**.

Derived from the Nature subject ontology (nature.com/subjects) merged with the Elsevier Journal Finder taxonomy in your screenshots.

**Why 6 and not 4:** the Elsevier screenshots use 4 areas, but they bury Earth/climate/geoscience inside "Physical Sciences and Engineering". That group owns a whole distinctive plot vocabulary — ternary diagrams, stereonets, concordia plots, Piper diagrams, Hovmoller sections, Taylor diagrams — none of which resemble physics or engineering plots. Splitting it out is what makes the ternary-plot gap you spotted impossible to repeat. The 6th area, *Cross-cutting Methods*, is a layer rather than a silo: ROC curves, confusion matrices, SHAP plots and loss curves belong to every area at once, so they carry an XM tag **plus** whichever domain tags apply.
